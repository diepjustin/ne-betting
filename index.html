Nebraska football prediction markets

A pipeline that records every trade on Nebraska football markets at Kalshi and Polymarket, so the question of how much money moves on them can be answered from evidence rather than estimated.

Two prediction markets list contracts on Nebraska football: who wins each game, the spread, the total, season win totals, conference and playoff outcomes, and markets on individual players. Both publish every completed trade. This project collects those trades daily and keeps each response exactly as the platform returned it, so any figure can be traced back to the response that produced it.

Nothing here is a finished analysis. This page reports what has been collected, not what it means.

These two columns are not comparable, and the totals should not be added. Three reasons, all of which matter:

  • The units differ. A Kalshi contract settles at $1, so contracts are a count, not dollars. The Polymarket figure is dollars actually paid by the trader who took each fill.
  • The windows differ. Kalshi serves recent trades from one endpoint and older ones from another, so the figure above starts in July 2026. Polymarket returned history back to 2024. A larger number here can mean a longer window rather than more trading.
  • Volume counts both sides. Every trade has a buyer and a seller. Neither figure is "money at risk on Nebraska," and neither is a count of people.

Where the numbers come from

Both platforms publish market data through public interfaces that need no account. The collectors discover Nebraska markets on each run rather than working from a fixed list, because both platforms add markets weekly. Every response is archived unmodified, with the time it was fetched, before anything is read from it.

Kalshi

Thirty-three college football market families are walked each run. A market counts as a Nebraska market if its identifier or its text names Nebraska, which includes the opponent's side of a Nebraska game. Ninety of the 228 markets have already settled.

Polymarket

Game markets are found through the college football listing and futures through keyword search, then filtered. Nebraska's basketball teams use the same abbreviation as its football teams, so an early version of this filter pulled in 67 basketball events, including a single basketball game that had traded $503,345. Events must now carry a football tag before anything else is considered.

What this does not cover

  • Sportsbooks. No sportsbook or daily fantasy prop lines are collected. Their interfaces refuse automated access, and the one open source is a weekly selection that did not list Nebraska at all. A sportsbook publishes a line and never publishes how much was bet on it.
  • Who is trading. Kalshi publishes no identity for the parties to a trade. Polymarket publishes a wallet address for each fill; those are recorded for analysis but are redacted from everything published here, because a wallet address is a person's trading history.
  • Player markets. Both platforms list markets on named Nebraska players. They are collected because they are markets, and they are reported only as totals. No figure is attached to any named athlete.

Method and source data
